# Emergency drill brief

Capability template, version 0.1.0.

## Purpose

Prepare the brief for one planned drill, such as a fire evacuation, from the site's own emergency arrangements: the one question the drill answers, the scenario the responsible person has approved, a run sheet, who does what and who observes, what each observer records, how earlier findings will be looked at again, and the questions for the debrief. Every gap becomes a question for the responsible person. The assistant prepares; it never writes or changes an emergency arrangement, approves a scenario, says what any law or regulator requires, announces or runs a drill, or judges whether a drill went well.

## When to use it and when not to

Use it once the date of a drill is set, usually a week or two ahead. Run it again if the date moves, a role-holder is absent, the scenario changes or an arrangement is corrected.

Do not use it:

- while an emergency is happening. Follow the site's own arrangements;
- to write or revise the emergency plan, or to choose routes, assembly points, alarms or roles. The responsible person owns those, and the brief only reads them;
- to say what a law, a regulator, an insurer or a landlord requires of the site, or how often drills must be held;
- to brief a drill that uses real fire, real smoke, live rescue, working at height, confined spaces or moving vehicles. That needs the exercise arrangements of a competent person, not this brief;
- to teach the arrangements to new people (that is an induction), or to plan how the business keeps working after a disruption (that is a continuity plan).

## Roles

- **Runs it:** the site manager, office manager or facilities lead.
- **Approves the scenario and the brief, authorizes the drill and accepts any resulting actions:** the responsible person for emergency arrangements, named by the organization. Always a person.
- **Leads the drill on the day:** the drill lead the responsible person names.
- **Holds each part in the plan:** the role-holders in the roles list, each with a deputy.
- **Observes:** the observers the responsible person names, who record and do not direct.
- **Receives the approved brief:** the people the responsible person decides.

## Inputs

The assistant works from notes and tables the person supplies. It needs no connection to any building system and never asks for a password or a login code.

1. **The drill details,** as lines of "label: value". Needed: site, drill type, date, start time, today's date, approver (by role), drill lead (by role). If any of these is missing, stop and ask. Also read when given: finish time, expected numbers on site on the day (any breakdown supplied), who is told in advance and who is not, targets (such as time to reach the assembly point), debrief date and time, the question the drill should answer, the scenario, whether the scenario is approved (by whom and when), what is pretended, what is real, the pretend conditions and who sets them, the alarm and who sounds it, the stop conditions, and the observers with the areas each watches. For each item that is missing write the wording in the output format ("finish not set", "not stated", "target not set") and continue, except for the scenario.
2. **The arrangements list.** Column headers: ID, Arrangement, Detail, Where written. One row for each arrangement the drill touches, such as the alarm, an exit, a route, the assembly point, the roll call and the all clear. Copied from the site's emergency plan, not invented. If the list is missing, or a column header does not match, stop and ask: arrangements are never supplied from the type of site.
3. **The roles list.** Column headers: ID, Plan role, Duty in the plan, Holder, Deputy. If it is missing, continue and write "roles not supplied" in the header; if only a header does not match, stop and ask.
4. **Absences,** as lines of person or role and dates. If missing, write "absences not supplied" in the header.
5. **Findings from earlier drills.** Column headers: Finding ID, Date, Finding, Action, Status. Status is done, open, or empty. If missing, write "no previous findings supplied".

Where the inputs name a person in connection with a health condition or a disability, refer to them by role or number only, and ask that the detail be removed from the source.

## The procedure

1. **Check the inputs.** Confirm one drill, one date and one site. Count the arrangements, roles and findings. If two rows share an ID, stop and ask which is which. If a column header does not match, stop and ask.
2. **Check the scenario.** If no scenario is supplied, do not write the brief. Offer up to three options in the scenario-options format, each pretending one condition and naming the arrangement or earlier finding it tests, and stop. If the scenario asks for real fire, real smoke, live rescue, working at height, a confined space or moving vehicles, decline that part, say why, and ask the responsible person for a scenario that only pretends it. Write the scenario as supplied, without adding facts. If it is not marked approved by a named role, print NOT APPROVED in the header and still prepare the brief.
3. **State the question.** Use the question supplied. If none is supplied, take the oldest open earlier finding, or if there is none, the arrangement the scenario puts under most strain, and say which in the Source. One question only. If the person supplied several, ask which one the drill answers.
4. **Build the run sheet.** Number the steps in the order they happen: any pretend set-up, the alarm, each route used, arrival at the assembly point, the roll call, the all clear. Give a time only where one was supplied or can be added directly: a start time plus a supplied target in minutes. Otherwise write "From the alarm" or "After the roll call". Each step carries "By: [role]." naming who does it (for a step everyone on site does, write "By: everyone on site") and "Arrangement: [ID]", citing an arrangement ID. A step whose arrangement is not on the list is written with "arrangement not supplied" and counted as a gap. Add no step that is not in an arrangement, the scenario or the drill details.
5. **Check the arrangements list for gaps.** Look for these arrangements: alarm, exit, route, assembly point, roll call, all clear, and help for people who cannot use the stairs or need assistance to leave. List any that is absent under the gaps heading, one flag each. Look for two rows that give different details for the same thing, such as two assembly points for the same area, and flag the conflict naming both IDs. The gaps section holds only flags, or "none"; do not add a line saying the other arrangements are present.
6. **List the roles.** Print each role with its duty, holder and deputy exactly as supplied, then the status on the date. Flag: no holder named; holder away on the drill date (say whether the deputy is named and not reported away); deputy away on the drill date; holder and deputy the same person. A deputy of "none" is printed as given and is not flagged.
7. **List the observers.** Print each observer with the areas supplied. Flag an observer who holds or covers a duty in the roles list, naming every duty held or covered: one flag for each observer, however many duties. Flag an observation point that has no observer for its area. Take the area from the arrangement's detail; an arrangement heard or used in two areas goes to the observer of each. If the detail names no area, take the area of the step before it on the run sheet, and write "(area taken from the run sheet)" after the observer. If no area can be found, flag it. This step does not judge how much one observer carries, or whether two points happen at once in different places; the responsible person looks at that when approving.
8. **Make the observation points.** One for each arrangement in the list that the scenario or the run sheet uses, numbered O01 onward in the list's order. Each states what to watch, what to record and who observes, using only the arrangement's detail, the supplied targets and the supplied expected numbers. Where a previous finding bears on the arrangement, name it; its action may supply a watch item, and the item then says "(from [finding ID])". Record measures, such as a time, a count or a yes or no, never a verdict and never a description: where something would be described, record how many people or how many times instead. Expected numbers are compared with counts as supplied; an absence is set against them only where the inputs say whom the numbers include. Whether a set-up step could tip off people who are not told is for the responsible person; the brief prints who is told, as supplied.
9. **Place the earlier findings.** For each earlier finding, name the observation point that will look at it again. A finding with status done is looked at to see whether the fix holds; an open one is looked at as still open. A finding with no status is printed as "not stated" and flagged. If no observation point can look at a finding, write "cannot be seen on this drill" and make it a decision.
10. **Write the debrief questions.** One question for each observation point, in order, each citing its ID and phrased as something people can answer from what they saw or did. Then three standing questions: what happened that nobody planned for, what people found confusing, and which observations need an action, who owns each and by when, with the responsible person deciding. Quote targets and counts only as supplied.
11. **Write the decisions.** One numbered decision for each flag, addressed to the responsible person: the facts, what depends on it, and the question. Order them by where the flag stands in the brief: the gaps section, then roles, then observers, then earlier findings. Recommend nothing unless asked.
12. **Return the draft** to the person who ran it, for the responsible person's approval. A person approves it, announces the drill to those who are to be told and runs it. After the drill, the observers' notes and the debrief are for the responsible person; the assistant can lay out the notes against the observation points if asked, and it still judges nothing.

## Exceptions and stop conditions

- **Drill type, date, site, approver or drill lead missing, or no arrangements list:** stop and ask.
- **No scenario:** return scenario options and stop.
- **Scenario involving real fire, real smoke, live rescue, working at height, confined spaces or moving vehicles:** decline that part and ask for a pretended version.
- **Asked to write, change or fill in an emergency arrangement, route, role or assembly point:** decline and name the responsible person. Record the gap as a decision.
- **Asked what the law or a regulator requires, or whether the drill satisfies it:** decline. The responsible person and their own advisers confirm that.
- **Asked to approve the scenario, announce the drill, tell anyone, or say a drill passed or failed:** decline and name the role that does it.
- **Asked to direct people during a live emergency:** decline. Follow the site's own arrangements.
- **Inputs contain a password, a login code or a person's health details:** do not repeat them; tell the person to remove them from the source.
- **Targets or numbers that contradict each other,** such as an expected total that does not equal its parts: show both as supplied and make it a decision. Do not choose one.

## Reviewer checklist

1. Every arrangement in the brief is on the supplied list and matches it; none has been added or changed. (Responsible person)
2. The scenario is as supplied, nothing in it asks for real fire, smoke, rescue, heights, confined spaces or vehicles, and its approval status is true. (Responsible person)
3. Every time on the run sheet is a supplied time or a start time plus a supplied target, and the arithmetic is right. (Drill lead)
4. Every role, holder and deputy matches the roles list, and each flag is true against the absences. (Site manager)
5. Every observation point records a measure, never a verdict, and has an observer for its area. (Drill lead)
6. Every earlier finding is placed at an observation point or marked as not visible, and each status is as supplied. (Responsible person)
7. The number of decisions equals the number of flags, and the counts in the header are right. (Site manager)
8. No sentence states what a law or regulator requires, and no arrangement has been invented. (Responsible person)
9. The brief is approved before the drill is announced. (Responsible person)

## Acceptance tests

Proposed tests. None has been run.

1. **Complete inputs.** All five inputs, an approved scenario, no absences. Expected: a full brief with every arrangement used observed, no role flags, and every figure traced to an input.
2. **No scenario.** Expected: three options in the scenario-options format, no brief.
3. **Scenario not approved.** Expected: NOT APPROVED in the header, the brief still prepared.
4. **Scenario with real smoke.** Expected: that part declined, a pretended version asked for.
5. **Holder away.** A role-holder is away on the date and the deputy is named. Expected: flagged, one decision, the deputy named as not reported away.
6. **Holder and deputy the same.** Expected: flagged; a deputy of "none" is not flagged.
7. **Observer with a duty.** Expected: flagged with the duties named, one decision.
8. **Missing arrangement.** No arrangement for help to leave. Expected: listed under the gaps, flagged, a decision; nothing invented.
9. **Conflicting rows.** Two assembly points for the same area. Expected: a conflict flag naming both IDs; neither chosen.
10. **Finding with no status.** Expected: "not stated", flagged, placed at an observation point.
11. **Finding that cannot be seen.** An action about a document held in an office. Expected: "cannot be seen on this drill" and a decision.
12. **Header mismatch.** The arrangements list has a column named "Notes" in place of "Detail". Expected: stops and asks.
13. **Totals that disagree.** Expected 30 with a breakdown of 26 + 7. Expected: both shown as supplied, a decision, no correction.
14. **Asked for a verdict or a legal statement.** "Did we pass?" or "Is this what the regulations require?" Expected: declines and names the role.
15. **Asked to write the plan.** Expected: declines, names the responsible person, records the gap.

## What to customize

- Your arrangements list: copy it from your own emergency plan, and keep the columns as written.
- Who approves a scenario, who leads a drill, and whether an observer may also hold a duty. As written, an observer who holds a duty is flagged.
- Your targets, such as time to the assembly point and time to complete the roll call. As written, a missing target is printed as "target not set".
- Which kinds of drill you brief with it, and which pretend conditions your site allows.
- How announced and unannounced drills are handled and who is told.
- Whether an unapproved scenario stops the brief. As written, it is prepared with NOT APPROVED in the header.
- The three standing debrief questions.
- Where the observers' notes and the debrief actions are kept, and who owns each action.
- Which files the assistant may read in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini.
