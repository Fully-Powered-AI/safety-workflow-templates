# Emergency drill brief

Before a fire, evacuation or other drill, the site or office manager gets one brief: the scenario the responsible person has approved, who does what, what each observer records, and the questions for the debrief. It writes no emergency plan.

*Workflow template · Health, safety and environment · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/emergency-drill-brief/)*

## The outcome

A drill is worth running only if it answers a question. The brief starts there: one question, taken from something the site wants to learn, such as whether a corrected arrangement now holds or whether everyone can be counted when one exit cannot be used.

Everything else follows from the site's own arrangements. The scenario is written up for the responsible person to approve. The run sheet puts the alarm, the routes, the assembly point and the roll call in order, with the times your site has set. Each person with a part in the drill appears with their deputy, and the page says plainly when a holder is away on the day or when one person has been given the observer's chair and a duty at once.

Observers go out with something to record, not a feeling to report: what to watch, where, and what to write down. Findings from the last drill come back as things to look at on this one. Afterwards the debrief has a question for every observation, ready to read aloud.

The AI assistant writes no emergency plan, says nothing about what any law requires, approves nothing and judges nothing. It prepares the brief; people run the drill and decide what it showed.

## Who starts the work, and when

The site manager, office manager or facilities lead starts it once the date of a drill is set, usually a week or two ahead, so that the responsible person can approve the scenario and the observers can be chosen. It can run again if the date moves, a role-holder is absent or the scenario changes.

The responsible person for emergency arrangements approves the scenario and the brief, and runs or authorizes the drill.

## What they supply

- **The drill details.** Site, type of drill, date and times, who approves, who leads, how many people are expected, who is told in advance, the targets your site has set, and when the debrief is.
- **The scenario,** in a few sentences, with what is pretended, what is real and when the drill stops. If you have not chosen one, the workflow template offers up to three options and stops until you do.
- **The site's arrangements.** Alarm, exits, routes, assembly point, roll call and all clear, as your emergency plan already describes them.
- **The roles.** Who holds each part in the plan, with a deputy, and who will observe.
- **Absences** around the date.
- **Findings from earlier drills,** with what was done about each.

## What comes out

One brief in a fixed order: the header and counts, the question the drill answers, the scenario, the run sheet, the gaps in the arrangements list, the roles and observers, the observation points, the earlier findings and where each will be looked at, the debrief questions, the decisions for the responsible person, what the brief does not cover, and a closing line that it is a draft for the responsible person's approval and that nothing has been announced, sent or run.

Every line traces to something you supplied. Where something was not supplied, the brief says so and asks. A person approves it before anything is announced or run.

## What has to change for your company

Use your own emergency plan as the only authority: the workflow template adds no arrangements of its own. Decide who approves a scenario and who leads a drill, and whether observers may also hold a duty. Set your targets, such as time to the assembly point and time to complete the roll call, or leave them out. Decide how announced or unannounced drills are handled and who is told. Agree where observation sheets are kept and who owns each action from the debrief. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, from notes you supply; it needs no connection to any building system.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
