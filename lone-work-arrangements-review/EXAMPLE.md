# Lone work arrangements review: an invented example

*Illustrative example with invented data.* The business, the sites, the people, the roles, the tests, the dates and every figure below are invented. The business is an invented building-maintenance company that looks after two buildings, Site A and Site B. Its operations manager runs the review. Its outside health and safety adviser is the competent person, and the facilities supervisor is the work's supervisor. People appear by role.

The inputs come first, in full, then the review the template returns.

## Inputs

### 1. Limits (typed by the operations manager)

```
Longest time between contacts: 60 minutes
Wait before acting on a missed check-in: 10 minutes
Longest time for a responder to reach the site: 30 minutes
Review prepared on: 9 November 2026
Competent person: outside health and safety adviser
Supervisor: facilities supervisor
```

### 2. Tasks (pasted from the rota sheet)

```
Task ID | Task | Site | Place | Who does it | Days and hours | Longest stretch alone (minutes) | Alone | Restriction from existing assessment
T1 | Read and reset meters | Site A | Plant room (basement) | Technician 1 | Monday to Friday 07:00 to 08:30 | 90 | Yes | None stated
T2 | Clear roof gutters | Site B | Roof | Technician 2 | Saturday 08:00 to 11:00 | 180 | Sometimes | Assessment of March 2026: "not to be done alone"
T3 | Evening lock-up and alarm set | Site A | Ground floor | Caretaker 1 | Monday to Friday 18:00 to 18:45 | 45 | Yes | None stated
T4 | Stores stocktake | Site A | Store room | Storekeeper and Technician 1 | First Monday of the month 10:00 to 12:00 | 120 | No | None stated
T5 | Fortnightly van check | Site A | Car park | Technician 2 | Friday 15:00 to 15:30 | 30 |  | None stated
```

### 3. Places (pasted from the operations manager's notes)

```
Site | Place | Means of contact | Coverage verified | Evidence and date
Site A | Plant room (basement) | Wall phone on the internal line | Yes | Tested by Supervisor 1 on 2026-10-05; mobile phones have no signal there
Site B | Roof | Mobile phone | No | Not tested
Site A | Ground floor | Mobile phone | Yes | Tested by Supervisor 2 on 2026-10-05
```

### 4. Responders

```
Role | Days and hours | Site | Minutes to reach | Verified
Supervisor 1 | Monday to Friday 07:00 to 17:00 | Site A | 10 | Yes
Supervisor 1 | Monday to Friday 07:00 to 17:00 | Site B | 25 | Yes
Supervisor 2 | Monday to Friday 09:00 to 19:00 | Site A | 15 | Yes
Supervisor 2 | Monday to Friday 09:00 to 19:00 | Site B | 20 | Yes
Duty contact | Saturday and Sunday 08:00 to 18:00, by phone | Site B |  | No
```

### 5. Existing procedure (typed by the operations manager)

```
T1: Technician 1 phones Supervisor 1 from the plant room wall phone on starting at 07:00 and again on finishing at 08:30. Nothing is written about a missed call.
T2: Technician 2 texts the duty contact on going up and again on coming down. If no text arrives, the duty contact rings Technician 2.
T3: Caretaker 1 texts Supervisor 2 on leaving at 18:45. If there is no text by 18:55, Supervisor 2 rings Caretaker 1 and, if there is no answer, goes to Site A.
```

## The review the template returned

```
LONE WORK ARRANGEMENTS REVIEW: the invented building-maintenance company, Sites A and B

For: outside health and safety adviser (competent person), with the facilities supervisor. Prepared 9 November 2026 from 5 tasks, 3 places, 5 responder rows and the existing procedure for 3 tasks.
Limits used: longest time between contacts 60 minutes; wait before acting on a missed check-in 10 minutes; longest time for a responder to reach the site 30 minutes. Source: supplied by the operations manager.
Tasks supplied: 5. Reviewed as lone work: 3. Not alone: 1. Alone not stated: 1. Findings: 8. Unresolved: 5.

Tasks not reviewed
T4, Stores stocktake: not done alone.
T5, Fortnightly van check: alone not stated.
U1. Whether Technician 2 does the van check alone is not stated. The operations manager can say.

T1: Read and reset meters
Where and when: Site A, plant room (basement); Monday to Friday 07:00 to 08:30; longest stretch alone 90 minutes. Alone: Yes.
Check-in now: 07:00 phone call from the plant room wall phone to Supervisor 1; 08:30 the same.
Longest gap between contacts: 90 minutes.
Communication: wall phone on the internal line at the plant room; verified Yes; tested by Supervisor 1 on 2026-10-05; mobile phones have no signal there.
If a check-in is missed (as written): nothing written; the limits above were used, so the first step falls at 08:40 (08:30 plus the 10-minute wait).
Longest time before a responder could be at the place, if every step works: 90 + 10 + 10 = 110 minutes, with Supervisor 1 (assumed: no responder is named, so the verified responder on duty at 08:40 with the fewest minutes).
Responder on duty: Supervisor 1, Monday to Friday 07:00 to 17:00, is on duty at 08:40 and at 08:50, the time of arrival; verified. Supervisor 2 starts at 09:00.
Findings: 3
F1. Gap too long: 90 minutes between contacts against a limit of 60, which is 30 over.
F2. No missed check-in step: nothing is written about what happens if the 08:30 call does not come, who acts or who goes to the plant room.
F3. One responder: Supervisor 1 is the only verified responder on duty at 08:40.
Unresolved: 0
None.
Alternatives:
a. (F1) Add a call at 07:45, which makes both gaps 45 minutes.
b. (F2) Write the step: who rings at 08:40 and who goes if there is no answer. A person has to be named for each.
c. (F3) Start the task at 09:00 or later, when both supervisors are on duty until 17:00; this needs the meter readings to allow it, which is not stated. Or add a second responder available from 07:00.

T2: Clear roof gutters
Where and when: Site B, roof; Saturday 08:00 to 11:00; longest stretch alone 180 minutes. Alone: Sometimes.
Check-in now: 08:00 text to the duty contact on going up; 11:00 text on coming down.
Longest gap between contacts: 180 minutes.
Communication: mobile phone at the roof; verified No; evidence: not tested (see U3).
If a check-in is missed (as written): if no text arrives at 11:00, the duty contact rings Technician 2. Nothing is written after an unanswered ring. No wait is stated, so 10 minutes was used: the ring is at 11:10.
Longest time before a responder could be at the place, if every step works: cannot be worked out because the minutes for the duty contact to reach Site B are not stated (see U5). It is at least 180 + 10 = 190 minutes, plus travel.
Responder on duty: duty contact, Saturday and Sunday 08:00 to 18:00, by phone; verified No (see U4); no verified responder on duty at 11:10. Supervisors 1 and 2 work Monday to Friday only.
Findings: 3
F4. Gap too long: 180 minutes between contacts against a limit of 60, which is 120 over.
F5. No missed check-in step: nothing is written after an unanswered ring, and no one is named to go to the roof.
F6. Existing restriction: the March 2026 assessment says "not to be done alone", and the task is listed as alone Sometimes.
Unresolved: 4
U2. When Technician 2 does this task alone, and when not. The operations manager can say.
U3. Mobile coverage on the roof has not been tested. Whoever tests it can say.
U4. The duty contact's availability on Saturdays has not been verified, so no verified responder is on duty at 11:10. The operations manager can say.
U5. The minutes for the duty contact to reach Site B are not stated. The operations manager can say.
Alternatives:
a. (F4, F5, F6) Do the task in pairs, so it is no longer done alone.
b. (F4) Contacts at 09:00 and 10:00 as well would make every gap 60 minutes, which is not over the limit; this only helps once U3 is settled.
c. (F5) Write the steps after an unanswered ring: who acts, after how long, and who goes to the roof. A person has to be named for each.
d. (U4, U5) Do the task on a weekday, starting at 09:00 or later and ending by 16:25, so that the ring (by 16:35) and Supervisor 1's arrival (25 minutes later, by 17:00) fall inside the supervisors' hours; both supervisors are verified for Site B. This needs roof access on weekdays, which is not stated.

T3: Evening lock-up and alarm set
Where and when: Site A, ground floor; Monday to Friday 18:00 to 18:45; longest stretch alone 45 minutes. Alone: Yes.
Check-in now: 18:45 text from Caretaker 1 to Supervisor 2 on leaving; no contact at the start, so the gap runs from 18:00.
Longest gap between contacts: 45 minutes.
Communication: mobile phone at the ground floor; verified Yes; tested by Supervisor 2 on 2026-10-05.
If a check-in is missed (as written): if there is no text by 18:55, Supervisor 2 rings Caretaker 1 and, if there is no answer, goes to Site A. The wait written is 10 minutes, the same as the limit.
Longest time before a responder could be at the place, if every step works: 45 + 10 + 15 = 70 minutes, with Supervisor 2.
Responder on duty: Supervisor 2, Monday to Friday 09:00 to 19:00, is on duty at 18:55, the time of the ring. Arrival is at 19:10, after those hours end. Supervisor 1 finished at 17:00.
Findings: 2
F7. Responder hours: Supervisor 2 would arrive at 19:10, which is 10 minutes after the hours end at 19:00, and no other responder is on duty.
F8. One responder: Supervisor 2 is the only verified responder on duty at 18:55.
Unresolved: 0
None.
Alternatives:
a. (F7) Finish the lock-up and send the text by 18:30, which means starting at 17:45: the ring is then at 18:40 and the arrival at 18:55, inside Supervisor 2's hours. This needs an earlier lock-up to be possible, which is not stated.
b. (F7, F8) Add a second responder whose hours run past 19:00, or extend Supervisor 2's hours. The competent person and the supervisor decide who.

Across the tasks: None.

Decisions for the outside health and safety adviser and the facilities supervisor: 3
D1. T1: Are these arrangements acceptable? F1 to F3. Decision: ______ By: ______ Date: ______
D2. T2: Are these arrangements acceptable? F4 to F6, U2 to U5. Decision: ______ By: ______ Date: ______
D3. T3: Are these arrangements acceptable? F7 and F8. Decision: ______ By: ______ Date: ______

Declined: None.

Draft for the competent person's decision. Nothing has been sent, arranged or changed.
It states no legal duty and certifies nothing.
```

Every figure traces to the limits and the lists above. The minutes are worked out as the longest gap between contacts, plus the wait, plus the minutes the responder needs to reach the site.
