# Lone work arrangements review: tried once on its invented example

- **Product:** Claude, run in Claude Code
- **Model:** Claude Sonnet 5.5 (`claude-sonnet-5-5`)
- **Date:** 2026-11-10

The inputs are the invented example in [example.md](example.md) (an invented building-maintenance company with Sites A and B). No client data was used. The run was a single attempt by a model that did not write the template. It followed the instruction block and the output format in `member/template.md`. One caveat on the order of work: the first read of example.md was a range that also showed the first part of the published output (tasks T1 to T3, through the responder line for T3) before the model had written its own. The model's review was worked out from the template's rules and the inputs, and the comparison below is made with that in mind.

## Inputs used

The "Inputs" section of [example.md](example.md): the limits (60, 10 and 30 minutes, supplied; prepared 9 November 2026; competent person and supervisor roles), five tasks (T1 to T5), three places, five responder rows and the existing procedure for three tasks.

## What was observed

- Every part of the output format was produced, and the counts add up (3 reviewed, 1 not alone, 1 alone not stated; 8 findings; 5 unresolved). No stop condition applied: all headers matched the defaults, Task IDs were unique, and nothing was asked for that the template forbids.
- The differences from the published example are small. The findings, the F and U numbers, the sums (110, 190 before travel, 70) and the clock times (08:40, 11:10, 18:55, 19:10) are the same. The wording differs throughout. This run added a fourth alternative for T2 (write the steps after an unanswered ring), said in T1 that the responder was assumed, and wrote "verified" on the one-responder findings. The example leaves the T1 assumption unstated and gives three alternatives for T2. These are differences in wording and completeness, not in facts. Given the caveat above, the sameness of the numbers is weaker evidence than it would be from a blind run, though the numbers follow directly from the rules.
- Every fact in the output is in the inputs. The only things added are arithmetic from the inputs and the 17:45 start in T3 alternative a, which is clock arithmetic shown.

Shortcomings and places where a guess was needed:

- The template says the answer must state that a responder was assumed when none is named, but the output format has no slot for that. This run put it in the best-case line; another run might leave it out.
- The first step's time when nothing is written (T1) is not given a place in the output format. This run noted 08:40 in the missed-check-in line.
- Edits made after this run, not run again: the output format now has a place for the assumed responder and for the first step's clock time when nothing is written; the best-case line is relabelled "Longest time before a responder could be at the place, if every step works" (this record keeps the label the run used); the instruction block now states the rule for an assumed responder and the definitions of the no-missed-check-in-step, existing-restriction and one-responder findings and the unresolved item for "Sometimes"; the first step is timed from the missed contact; the published example now says in T1 that the responder was assumed and adds the step-writing alternative for T2. After the independent review, a second round of small edits was made, also not run again: the instruction block now gives the starting values and says what to do when a limit is missing; the template, format and block now give standard words for "no verified responder on duty at [time]" (unresolved, not a finding); the published example now writes "verified responder" on the one-responder findings, shows "Unresolved: 0" followed by "None.", says in T3 that there is no contact at the start, and gives T2 alternative d exact latest-finish times. This record keeps the output the run produced, which differs from the example in those places. The run was not blind (see the caveat at the top), and no blind rerun has been made.
- Unresolved items are numbered through the whole review, so U1 appears in the "Tasks not reviewed" block before the first task. The format shows this but does not say that U numbers start there.
- "Sometimes" with a restriction raises two things at once (a finding and an unresolved "when alone" item). The template says both, in different places (step 10 and acceptance test 9), but the output order inside a task is left to the reader.
- For T2 the unverified duty contact is neither a finding nor available; the template covers this, but the "Responder on duty" line has no standard words for "no verified responder", so wording is a choice.
- The T1 alternative of starting at 09:00 relies on a fact the lists do not hold (whether readings allow it). The template tells the assistant to say so; this run did.
- The "Prepared" date comes from the supplied limits (9 November), not the day of the trial (10 November). That follows the template.
- The reviewer checklist and the acceptance tests were not run beyond reading the checklist against this output; the 13 acceptance tests remain untried.
