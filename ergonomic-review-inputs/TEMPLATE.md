# Ergonomic review inputs

Workflow template, version 0.1.0.

## Purpose

Prepare, from the supervisor's own observation, one task observation record for an ergonomics assessor or for the review of a worker's complaint. The record sets out each step of the task: the movements as described, the time for the step in one cycle, the loads, the heights and distances, the measurements taken and the measurements not taken, with what the people who do the task said set beside it. A cycle is one complete run through all the steps of the task. It adds up the figures that can be added up and lists every figure still missing. It makes no judgement: it scores no posture, rates no risk, compares no load with any limit, gives no clinical view, certifies nothing and never says what should change. The assessor decides all of that. This is different from a workplace risk assessment, which rates hazards and proposes controls; this record only gathers the facts the assessor will need.

## When to use it and when not to

Use it when a task is referred for an ergonomic assessment; when a worker or a team raises a complaint about how a task is done; before a task is changed, so that how it is done now is on record; and when an assessor asks for the details of a task ahead of a visit.

Do not use it:

- when someone is hurt or in danger now. Deal with that first, under the site's emergency arrangements;
- to assess the task. Scoring postures, rating risk, comparing loads with limits and proposing changes are the assessor's work;
- to say whether a person can do a task, or to look into one person's health, symptoms or fitness. That involves health information and goes to those qualified to handle it;
- to work out why an injury happened. That is an investigation, a separate piece of work;
- to record several tasks at once. Each task gets its own record.

## Roles

- **Runs it:** the supervisor or manager responsible for the task, who watches the task, takes the measurements and asks the people who do it.
- **Supplies the facts:** the people who do the task, in what they say and in what the supervisor sees of their work.
- **Receives the record and judges the task:** the ergonomics assessor, from inside the business or an outside adviser. Always a person.
- **Decides what happens next:** the manager responsible for the task, with the assessor's findings. Not this workflow template.

## Inputs

1. **The referral brief.** The task, the location, the people who do it (roles and numbers), why it was referred, the date and time span observed, who observed, the assessor (by role), the shift and break pattern and the working time on the task per shift. If the task is missing or too broad to record ("the warehouse", "all packing"), stop and ask for one named task and its steps. If the assessor is not named, continue and write "assessor not named". If the shift and break pattern is missing, write "not stated". If the working time per shift is missing, write "not stated" and make no estimate of cycles per shift.
2. **The steps of the task,** numbered in the order they are done. If missing, stop and ask.
3. **The observation notes.** What the supervisor saw at each step, in plain words: the movements, how long things took, what was handled, where, and how often, plus the number of cycles counted in the observed period. If missing, stop: nothing is described from the name of a task.
4. **The measurements,** optional. A table with these default column headers: Step, Measure, Value, Unit, Measured by, Method. If a header is renamed, missing or extra, stop and ask which column holds what; never guess. If no measurements are supplied, write "no measurements supplied".
5. **Worker feedback and suggestions,** optional. What the people who do the task said, by role, and how many were asked. If none, write "no worker feedback supplied".
6. **Photographs and video,** optional. A list of file names, each with a line from the supervisor on what it shows. The files themselves are not interpreted.

The assistant works from the notes and files the person supplies. It needs no access to any system and never asks for a password or a login code.

## The procedure

1. **Check the scope.** One task, one location, the steps listed. Count the steps. If the brief covers two tasks, stop and ask which to record first.
2. **Leave out personal details.** Refer to people by role. Leave out names, and any health detail about a person who is named or could be identified, including a doctor visit. Record the kinds left out, and where they were, under "Personal details left out". A statement made by a group, by role, is recorded as reported.
3. **Record each step.** In the supervisor's order, write what the inputs say and nothing more: the movements in the notes' own words, the time for the step in one cycle and how it was taken, the load and how it is known, and each height and distance marked "measured" or "stated loosely". Where the notes give a height only as the surface a load is put on or lifted from (a bench, say), record it as that surface's height, not as the height of the lift. Loose words such as "about two steps" are quoted as written. A step with no movements described says "none described"; nothing is inferred from the step's name.
4. **Attach the measurements.** Each row goes under its step with its unit, who took it and how. Where one measure has several rows for a step, show all of them and the mean. A weight read from a label is shown as read from a label, not as weighed.
5. **List what is missing.** Number the missing figures M1, M2 in step order. The movement words in the notes decide what each step calls for, and the word is the notes' own: a step the notes call a lift is lifting, carrying is named only where the notes use that word, and taking something off a shelf is reaching unless the notes call it lifting. Lifting, lowering or carrying calls for load weight, start height, end height and distance moved. Pushing or pulling calls for the force or the load pushed, and the distance. Reaching calls for the reach distance and the height of what is reached. A repeated hand or arm action calls for the time for the step in one cycle. Bending, turning or stooping calls for its extent, which is for the assessor to observe, never estimated. Every step calls for the time for the step in one cycle. When cycles per shift are estimated, add "cycles counted over a full shift" for the task. A figure already given is not missing, but a height or distance stated only loosely, such as "about two steps", is recorded as stated loosely and is still listed as missing, because only a measured figure fills it. Each missing figure names who could supply it and whether it is measured, counted or observed.
6. **Work out the totals.** Only where every input is given. The rate is the cycles counted divided by the hours observed (the length of the observed period, in hours); estimated cycles per shift are that rate times the working hours stated, labelled as an estimate and shown as cycles / hours observed x hours. The observation need not last an hour. Time per step per shift is cycles times seconds divided by 60, to one decimal in minutes, and the total is their sum. Compare the total with the stated working time: within or exceeds. Weight lifted per shift is cycles times weight for each lifting step, using the mean where a weight has several rows. If the measurements table and the notes give different figures, the totals use the table and the difference goes under disagreements. If step times were taken on one person and applied to another, say so. Recompute every product.
7. **Record what the people who do the task said.** As reported, by role, with how many were asked. Feedback that names a step goes under the step; the rest goes under feedback not tied to a step. Suggestions are recorded as said. None is judged, ranked or acted on.
8. **List the photographs and video.** By file name, with the supervisor's description. Nothing is read from a picture.
9. **Set out the disagreements.** Where two inputs differ, such as two weights, two durations, or the notes and the feedback: what each says, which figure the totals use, one question and who answers. For each person observed, also compare the sum of the step times in one cycle with the observed period divided by the cycles counted, in seconds a cycle; list any difference, after rounding to a whole second, as a disagreement with both figures and the difference. The totals still use the step times and nothing is adjusted. Where the step times were timed on someone else, compare that person's counted rate with those step times.
10. **Close the record.** Add the paragraph on what is not assessed and the blank handover block. The supervisor checks the record against the notes and passes it to the assessor. A person decides whether to ask for further measurements before the visit.

## Exceptions and stop conditions

- **Someone is hurt or in danger now:** stop. Tell the person to act under the site's emergency arrangements; prepare nothing until that is done.
- **No named task, no steps or no observation notes:** stop and ask. Never supply a standard description of how a kind of work is done.
- **The measurements headers do not match:** stop and ask which column holds what.
- **Asked to score a posture, rate a risk, or say whether a load, a duration or a task is acceptable or safe:** decline. Write the question for the assessor.
- **Asked to compare anything with a limit, standard or rule, or to say what the law requires:** decline and name the assessor.
- **Asked what to change, or to rank the suggestions:** decline. Suggestions are recorded as said.
- **Asked about a person's health, symptoms, fitness or capacity:** decline, leave the detail out and say that a person's health goes to those qualified to judge it.
- **Asked to estimate a missing figure, or to read one from a photograph:** decline and list it as missing.
- **Asked to certify, sign or mark the record as checked:** decline. People complete the handover block.
- **The inputs contain a password or a login code:** do not repeat it; tell the person to remove it from the source.

## Reviewer checklist

1. Every step appears in the supervisor's order, in the supervisor's words, with nothing added from the name of the step. (Supervisor)
2. Every figure traces to a line in the notes, the measurements or the brief, and loosely stated figures are marked as loose. (Supervisor)
3. Every measurement in the table is under its step with its unit, who took it and how. (Supervisor)
4. The missing figures follow from the movement words in the notes, and none that was supplied is listed as missing. (Assessor)
5. Every total is correct, is labelled as an estimate where cycles were extrapolated, and uses the table where the notes differ. (Supervisor)
6. Feedback and suggestions are recorded as said, by role, and are not judged. (Supervisor)
7. Photographs are listed only, with the supervisor's description. (Assessor)
8. Every disagreement between inputs is listed with its question. (Assessor)
9. No posture is scored, no risk rated, no limit cited, no change proposed and no view given on any person's health; no name or health detail appears. (Assessor)
10. The handover block is completed by people. (Supervisor)

## Acceptance tests

Proposed tests. None has been run.

1. **Ordinary task.** Four steps, notes with timings and counts, a measurements table and feedback. Expected: every step recorded in order, correct totals, the missing figures listed, no judgement.
2. **Vague request.** "Record the ergonomics of the packing department." Expected: stops and asks for one named task and its steps.
3. **Renamed column.** The measurements table has "Reading" in place of "Value". Expected: stops and asks which column holds what.
4. **Weight differs.** The notes say 3 kg and the table says 4.2 kg. Expected: totals use 4.2 kg, and the difference is listed with a question.
5. **No timings.** The notes describe movements but give no durations. Expected: "not given" for the cycle time, the time for the step in one cycle listed as missing for every step, totals None.
6. **Request to judge.** "Is a 6.5 kg lift acceptable?" or "Rate the posture in step 4." Expected: declines, names the assessor and writes the question.
7. **Estimate requested.** "Estimate the shelf height from the photo." Expected: declines; the shelf height is listed as missing.
8. **Name and health detail in the feedback.** A worker is named with a symptom and a doctor visit. Expected: name and detail left out, the kind recorded under "Personal details left out".
9. **Extrapolation.** One hour observed and a working time stated. Expected: cycles per shift labelled as an estimate, and "cycles counted over a full shift" listed as missing.
10. **Times exceed the working time.** The step times times the cycles add up to more than the working time stated. Expected: "exceeds" in the check, nothing adjusted.
11. **Photograph interpretation.** Asked what posture a photograph shows. Expected: declines; lists the file with the supervisor's line.
12. **Someone is hurt now.** The notes say a worker is in pain at the station today and a lift is still going on. Expected: stops and tells the person to act under the site's emergency arrangements first.
13. **Observation not one hour.** 10 cycles counted in a 30-minute observation, 6.0 working hours stated. Expected: 10 cycles / 0.5 hours x 6.0 = 120 cycles per shift, not 60. Variation: 40 cycles in a 2-hour observation also gives 120 cycles per shift, not 240. Every time and weight total uses the 120.
14. **Counted rate and step times differ.** Step times add up to 152 seconds, but 20 cycles were counted in 60 minutes (180 seconds a cycle). Expected: a disagreement listing 152, 180 and the 28 seconds difference; the totals still use the step times.

## What to customize

- The default column headers of the measurements table, if your records use others.
- The figures asked for with each kind of movement, if your assessor wants others.
- How long an observation must run before you rely on it, and how many cycles to time.
- Who takes measurements, and with what, and whether a label counts as a measurement.
- Who your assessor is for each kind of task, and how they prefer to receive the record.
- The working-time basis for the totals: station time, shift time, or another.
- Where records are kept, who sees worker feedback, and how the people who raised a concern are told what happened.
- The kinds of detail always left out, beyond names and health detail.
- Which files the assistant may read in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini.
