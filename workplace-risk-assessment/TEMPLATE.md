# Workplace risk assessment

Workflow template, version 0.1.0.

## Purpose

Prepare the written risk assessment for one task or one workplace from the manager's own notes and records: the steps of the work, each hazard and how it would hurt someone, who could be harmed, the controls already in place, a likelihood-and-severity rating with those controls, further controls listed from the most effective down, each with an owner and a date, and the rating expected once they are in place. Every gap becomes a question, and the draft goes to a competent person, who reviews it and signs it. The assistant prepares; it never names an owner, picks a date, signs the assessment, stops or restarts work, orders anything or sends anything.

## When to use it and when not to

Use it before a new task starts or an existing one changes; when the equipment, the materials, the people or the place change; when an incident or near miss shows the current assessment missed something; and on the review date.

Do not use it:

- when someone is in danger now. Deal with that first, under the site's emergency arrangements;
- to work out why an incident happened and what to correct afterwards. That is an investigation, a separate piece of work; its findings can feed the next run of this one;
- as the specialist assessment a hazard may need, such as measuring noise or dust, examining lifting equipment, checking a structure or assessing a building for fire. The assessment says the specialist work is needed and leaves the figures to a qualified person;
- to assess one named person's health or fitness for a task. That involves health information and goes to the people qualified to handle it;
- to say what the law requires in a particular country or sector. The competent person confirms which duties apply.

## Roles

- **Runs it:** the site manager, office manager or operations manager responsible for the task or the place.
- **Supplies the facts:** the people who do the work and their supervisor, in the walk-round and the conversation.
- **Names each owner and date:** the manager who runs it.
- **Reviews, confirms or changes the ratings, decides whether a task runs, signs:** the competent person, someone with the training and experience to judge the hazards of this work, from inside or outside the business. Always a person.
- **Owns each further control:** the role the manager names.
- **Receives the signed assessment:** the people who do the work, and anyone else the business decides.

## Inputs

1. **The scope brief.** The task or workplace, the location, the people who do the work (roles and numbers), others who may be affected, how often it happens, the assessment date, who runs it, the competent person (by role) and the review interval. If the task or the location is missing or too broad to assess ("the warehouse", "general safety"), stop and ask for one named task and its steps. If the competent person is not named, continue, write "competent person not named" and raise a decision. If the review interval is missing, write "review date not set" and raise a decision. If how often is missing, write "not stated".
2. **The steps of the work,** numbered in the order they are done. For a workplace rather than a task, the areas or activities instead. If missing, stop and ask.
3. **The walk-round and conversation notes.** What was seen, what the people doing the work say goes wrong, the controls in place, any rating the manager proposes, further controls already agreed with their owners and dates, and any costs known. If missing, stop: hazards are never guessed from the name of a task.
4. **The incident and near-miss log** for this task or place, optional. A table with these column headers: Date, Location, Task or step, What happened, Harm, Reported by. If a header is renamed, missing or extra, stop and ask which column holds what; never guess. If no log is supplied, write "no incident log supplied".
5. **Other records,** optional: the previous assessment, inspection reports, product safety data sheets, training records. Use only what they say. Name each one used under Sources, or write "no other records supplied".
6. **The rating scale:** the organization's likelihood and severity scales, how the score is worked out, the bands and what each band requires. If missing, use the default scale below and say so.
7. **The High deadline:** how many days after the assessment date further controls for a High or Very high hazard must be in place. If missing, use 30 days and say so.

**The default scale.** Likelihood: 1 Very unlikely, 2 Unlikely, 3 Possible, 4 Likely, 5 Very likely. Severity: 1 Slight, first aid at most; 2 Minor, off work up to three days; 3 Serious, off work longer, or an injury that heals; 4 Major, permanent injury or long-term illness; 5 Fatal, or several people seriously hurt. Score: likelihood multiplied by severity. Bands and what each requires:

- **Low, 1 to 3:** keep the existing controls.
- **Medium, 4 to 8:** further controls, each with an owner and a date.
- **High, 9 to 14:** further controls, each with an owner and a date no later than the High deadline.
- **Very high, 15 to 25:** as for High, and the competent person decides whether and how the task runs before it next runs.

The assistant works from the notes and files the person supplies. It needs no access to any system and never asks for a password or a login code.

## The procedure

1. **Check the scope.** One task or workplace, one location, the steps listed. Count the steps. If the brief covers two tasks, stop and ask which to assess first; each gets its own assessment.
2. **Leave out personal details.** Refer to people by role. Leave out names, and any health detail about a person who is named or could be identified. Record the kinds left out, and where they were, under "Personal details left out". A log entry that names nobody is used as written.
3. **Find the hazards, step by step.** From the notes and records only, list each thing that could cause harm and the event by which it would. One hazard per line, numbered H1, H2 in the order of the steps, by the first step at which each occurs; the same hazard at two steps is one line naming both. Never add a hazard the inputs do not support. Where the work plainly suggests a hazard the notes do not mention, put it under "Things to check before sign-off" as a question. Every step either carries a hazard or is listed under "Steps with no significant hazard found in the notes".
4. **Who could be harmed.** For each hazard, the roles and numbers from the scope brief, plus visitors, drivers, contractors or the public where the notes place them near it.
5. **Existing controls.** Only those the notes or records say are in place, described as loosely as the notes describe them ("most trailers", "on request"). If none, write "none recorded".
6. **Rate before further controls,** with the existing controls only. If the manager gives a rating, use it and mark it "as rated by [role]". Otherwise propose one and mark it "proposed". Either way, give a one-line reason drawn from the inputs: how often, what the log shows, how many people. The inputs rarely say how badly someone would be hurt; where they do not, judge the severity from the kind of event, such as a person struck by a vehicle or a load falling on someone, and write "severity [n] proposed from the kind of event" in the reason. Multiply, read the band from the scale and check the arithmetic.
7. **List the further controls,** most effective first, using these five levels: 1 Remove the hazard; 2 Replace it with something less harmful; 3 Engineer it out: guard, separate, enclose, secure or change the equipment; 4 Procedures: rules, training, signs, supervision; 5 Protective equipment. Write each control's level by its name: Remove, Replace, Engineer, Procedures or Protective equipment. Take every further control in the notes, with its owner and date. Propose more only where your after rating with the notes' controls alone (step 8) is still High or Very high, where none of them is at levels 1 to 3, or, for a High or Very high hazard, where a control from the notes has no date or a date after the High deadline; in that last case propose one interim control that can be in place at once and mark it "interim, until A[n] is in place". Each control carries its level, an owner (from the notes, or "owner to be named"), a date (from the notes, or "date not set"), where it came from ("from the notes" or "proposed by the assistant") and its action number. Never name an owner or choose a date. If every further control for a hazard is at level 4 or 5, add "Why nothing higher:" with the reason the notes give, or "no reason given".
8. **Rate after further controls:** the rating expected once every further control is in place, always marked "proposed", with a reason. Interim controls do not count: they hold the line only until the control they stand in for is in place. Where you proposed a control because the notes' controls alone would leave the hazard High or Very high, give that rating in the reason too ("with the notes' controls alone: [n] x [n] = [score], [band]"), so the competent person can see why the control was added. Severity moves only when a control reduces how badly someone could be hurt, such as a lower drop or a lighter load; otherwise only likelihood moves. A rating never falls unless a further control explains it.
9. **Flag each hazard** where any of these hold, all kinds on one flag line, counted once:
   - **Very high before further controls.**
   - **Still High** or **still Very high** after further controls.
   - **No owner** or **no date** on a further control. A control proposed by the assistant always carries "owner to be named" and "date not set", so it always raises this flag: the manager names both, or drops the control.
   - **Later than the High deadline:** for a High or Very high hazard, a control dated after the deadline.
   - **No reason given:** every further control is at level 4 or 5 and the notes say nothing about why.
   - **Specialist assessment needed:** the hazard needs a measurement or examination the inputs do not contain. Never estimate the figure.
10. **Build the action plan.** Every further control, numbered A1, A2 in hazard order, with its hazard, owner, date, source and the kinds of flag on that action, or "no flag".
11. **Write the decisions.** One numbered decision per flagged hazard, in hazard order, then one each for "competent person not named", "review date not set" and any request to lower a rating that no further control explains, if they apply. Each gives the facts and one question per kind of flag, and says who answers: the manager for owners and dates, the competent person for ratings, deadlines and whether the task runs meanwhile. Recommend nothing beyond the controls already listed.
12. **Things to check before sign-off.** Facts the notes state loosely, facts resting on a record that was not supplied, and hazards the work suggests that the notes do not mention. Each as a question naming the input it comes from. If none, write "none".
13. **Set the review.** The next review date is the assessment date plus the review interval. Add the standing reasons to review sooner.
14. **Close the draft.** Add the line on legal duties and the blank sign-off block, and return the draft to the manager, who passes it to the competent person. The competent person settles the decisions, confirms or changes every rating, and signs. A person shares the signed assessment with the people who do the work.

## Exceptions and stop conditions

- **Someone is in danger now:** stop. Tell the person to act under the site's emergency arrangements; prepare nothing until that is done.
- **No named task, no steps or no notes:** stop and ask. Never supply a standard list of hazards for a kind of work.
- **The log's column headers do not match:** stop and ask which column holds what.
- **Asked to sign, approve or mark the assessment as reviewed:** decline. Only the competent person signs.
- **Asked to choose an owner or a date, order equipment, contact a supplier, tell staff, or stop or restart work:** decline and name the role that does it.
- **Asked to lower a rating with no further control to explain it:** decline. Keep the rating and add the request to the decisions for the competent person.
- **Asked what the law requires, or to cite a law, regulation or standard:** decline. Write the question for the competent person.
- **A hazard needs a figure the records do not give,** such as a noise level, a dust reading or a safe load: flag "specialist assessment needed"; never estimate it.
- **The inputs hold health information about a named person, or ask for one person's fitness to work:** leave the detail out, note the kind left out, and say that a person's health and fitness go to those qualified to judge them.
- **The inputs contain a password or a login code:** do not repeat it; tell the person to remove it from the source.

## Reviewer checklist

1. Every step of the work appears, either with its hazards or under "no significant hazard found". (Competent person)
2. Every hazard comes from the notes or records; nothing has been added from the name of the task. (Manager who ran it)
3. Existing controls are only those actually in place, described no more firmly than the notes describe them. (Supervisor)
4. Every score is likelihood multiplied by severity, in the right band, and every rating has a reason. (Competent person)
5. No rating falls without a further control to explain it, and severity moves only where a control reduces how badly someone could be hurt. (Competent person)
6. Each hazard's further controls start at the highest level that is practicable, and any hazard controlled only by procedures or protective equipment says why. (Competent person)
7. Every further control has an owner and a date, or a flag and a decision; no owner or date was chosen by the assistant. (Manager who ran it)
8. Dates for High and Very high hazards fall within the High deadline, or are flagged. (Manager who ran it)
9. No law, regulation or standard is cited, no measurement is estimated, and no name or personal health detail appears. (Competent person)
10. The people who do the work have read their steps and recognize the hazards. (Supervisor)
11. The sign-off block is completed by the competent person before the assessment is shared. (Competent person)

## Acceptance tests

Proposed tests. None has been run.

1. **Ordinary task.** Four steps, notes, a log and agreed controls with owners and dates inside the deadline. Expected: every step covered, every score correct, no flags, the default scale named.
2. **Vague request.** "Do a risk assessment for the warehouse." Expected: stops and asks for one named task and its steps; lists no hazards.
3. **Renamed log column.** The log has "Injury" in place of "Harm". Expected: stops and asks which column holds what.
4. **Very high hazard.** A hazard rated 15 before further controls. Expected: flagged "Very high before further controls", with a decision for the competent person on whether and how the task runs meanwhile.
5. **Control dated after the deadline.** A High hazard's control falls six days after the High deadline. Expected: flagged "later than the High deadline", one interim control proposed and marked "interim, until A[n] is in place", the owner and date of the interim control left open.
6. **Protective equipment only.** The notes control a noise hazard with ear plugs alone and give no reason. Expected: a higher-level control is proposed, or "Why nothing higher: no reason given" is flagged; "specialist assessment needed" if the noise level is not in the records; no level estimated.
7. **Rating that falls with nothing added.** The notes give a lower after rating but no further control. Expected: the after rating stays equal to the before rating and the request goes to the decisions.
8. **Hazard the notes miss.** A task that includes working from a ladder, with nothing in the notes about falls. Expected: no hazard added; a question under "Things to check before sign-off".
9. **Name and injury in the notes.** "Sam hurt his knee and was off for two weeks." Expected: the name and the injury detail left out, the kind recorded under "Personal details left out".
10. **Asked to sign or cite the law.** "Sign it off as reviewed" or "Which regulation does this satisfy?" Expected: declines, names the competent person, writes the question as a decision.
11. **No competent person named and no review interval.** Expected: "competent person not named" and "review date not set" in the draft, each with its own decision.
12. **Someone in danger now.** The notes say a guard is off a running machine today. Expected: stops and tells the person to act under the site's emergency arrangements first.

## What to customize

- Your rating scale: the likelihood and severity steps, how the score is worked out, the bands and what each band requires. The default is a five-by-five scale.
- [30 days]: your High deadline.
- Who your competent person is for each kind of work, and whether that person is inside the business or an outside adviser.
- Who names owners and dates, and who may approve a date later than the deadline.
- Your review interval, and any reasons to review sooner beyond the standing ones.
- The column headers of your incident and near-miss log, if they differ from the defaults.
- The kinds of work you always send for a specialist assessment, such as noise, dust, lifting equipment or fire.
- Where signed assessments are kept, and how the people who do the work are shown theirs.
- The duties that apply where you operate, confirmed by the competent person; the template cites none.
- Which files the assistant may read in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini.
