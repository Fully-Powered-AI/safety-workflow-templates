# Workplace risk assessment: trial on the invented example

- **Product:** Claude, run in Claude Code
- **Model:** Claude Opus 5.5 (model ID claude-opus-5-5)
- **Date:** 2026-11-10

The inputs are the invented example in [public/example.md](example.md): the scope brief, the four steps, the walk-round notes and the four-row incident log, with no other records and no High deadline given. No client data was used.

This is the second run. The first, earlier on 10 November 2026 with Claude Sonnet 5.5, was done by an assistant that read the published example output only after it ran. It produced a complete draft and found ambiguities that an independent review then confirmed. Its output is not published. The template was revised after that review: the level of each control is written by its name; hazards are numbered in the order of the steps; interim controls do not count toward the after rating; a control proposed because the notes' controls alone would leave a hazard High or Very high now shows that rating in its reason; a control proposed by the assistant always raises the "no owner" and "no date" flag; a severity judged from the kind of event is allowed, and marked so; and the pasted instructions now carry who could be harmed, what each band requires, the full list of things to check and the stop for one person's health or fitness to work.

This run was done by the assistant that made those revisions, which had read the first run, the review and the published example before it ran. It is therefore not an independent run. It followed the revised instruction block and output format (version 0.1.0) on the example's inputs only and produced the draft below. The published example output was then brought into line with the revised template on four points (the title, who could be harmed, the severity reasons, and an after rating for H2 that had leaned on its interim control); where this run's judgment differs from the example, the difference is listed below rather than removed.

## What was observed

**Every required part was produced.** The header with its counts, four hazards in step order, who could be harmed, existing controls, before and after ratings each with a reason, further controls with level name, owner, date, source and action number, three flags, an eight-line action plan, three decisions, seven questions to check, the review date, the personal details left out, the legal-duties line, the blank sign-off block and the closing line. Nothing in the inputs triggered a stop condition. The arithmetic was checked: 3 x 5 = 15, 2 x 5 = 10, 2 x 5 = 10, 4 x 3 = 12, 1 x 5 = 5, 2 x 3 = 6 and 3 x 3 = 9, each in its band; 9 December 2026 is 30 days after 9 November 2026, and 15 December 2026 is six days after that.

**The first run's ambiguities, as handled by the revised template.**

- Level: written by name (Engineer, Procedures), as the output format now says.
- Hazard order: numbered by step, so H2 is the curtain (step 2) and H3 the forklift (step 3).
- Severity: the inputs never say how badly someone would be hurt by a trailer, a pallet or a forklift. Each severity of 5 was judged from the kind of event and the reason says so; the lifting hazard keeps the manager's own 3.
- Interim controls: left out of every after rating. For H2 the reason now rests on the strapped pallets alone.
- Proposed controls: every one raised "no owner" and "no date", as the template now says, and each went into a decision for the site manager.
- Extra control: for H4 this run rated the lift table alone at 3 x 3 = 9, High, because the bags would still be lowered to the bottom shelf, where the logged shoulder strain happened. It therefore proposed a waist-height shelf (A7) and gave the notes-only rating in the reason, as the revised template asks. The published example rates the lift table alone at 2 x 3 = 6 and proposes nothing more. The rule is now visible but still depends on the assistant's own rating; the competent person decides.
- Default deadline: the Inputs section now reads "30 days" without brackets, and it was applied as the default without question.

**Where the instructions still left a judgment to the assistant.**

- Likelihood is a judgment. This run gave the curtain and the forklift a 2, from one fall with no harm and no forklift incident in the log; the first run gave both a 3.
- Who could be harmed: the rule takes roles from the scope brief, plus drivers or visitors only where the notes place them near the hazard. The notes place nobody but staff on foot in the reversing area, so this run left visiting drivers out of H1, and left the marshal out of H2 because the notes put only the driver at the curtain. "Staff crossing the yard" has no count in the brief and was written "number not stated".
- Things to check: the template now lists loosely stated facts and facts resting on records that were not supplied. That brought in the bag weight from an unsupplied delivery note and the induction training, and made seven questions where the example has three. It is a matter of judgment which loose phrases are worth a question.
- The request to the supplier (A3) is a request to someone outside the business. It was kept as a control with its owner left open; the assistant contacted no one.

**Stops.** None. The instructions were sufficient to produce a draft without asking the user anything.

**Differences from the published example output.**

- One extra proposed action for H4, the waist-height shelf, so 8 actions (4 from the notes, 4 proposed) against 7 (4, 3), and decision 3 covers three actions instead of two. The interim lifting control is A8 here, A7 in the example.
- Seven questions to check against three: this run adds who marshals when the supervisor is busy, who checks the load and whether that means climbing onto the trailer, the bag weight from the delivery note, and the induction training.
- The interim controls are worded differently: for H1 this run keeps everyone on foot out of the reversing area, where the example has the marshal stand where the driver can see; for H2 this run closes the curtain again and leaves the decision to the warehouse supervisor, where the example names the marshal.
- Ratings, hazard order, flags, counts of hazards, flagged hazards and decisions, the High deadline and the review date match.

**Facts in either output that are not in the inputs.**

- This run: every severity of 5 (marked as judged from the kind of event); the notes-only rating for H4 and the reason that the bags would still be lowered to the bottom shelf; the interim and extra controls (all marked proposed).
- The example: every severity of 5 (marked the same way); the interim controls (marked proposed).

**Overall.** The revised template produced a complete, well-formed draft that obeyed its stop and decline rules and kept every decision with a person. The changes after review removed the formatting and ordering differences the first run found. Ratings and the choice of an extra control still rest on judgment, now marked as such, so two runs on the same inputs can differ in a number or an extra action; a competent person confirms every rating. This was one run, by the assistant that revised the template, on one invented example; it is not a test in a client environment.
